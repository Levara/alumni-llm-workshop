# Why Hezbollah Fighters Are Embracing an Unpopular and Costly War with Israel

**Source:** The Christian Science Monitor
**Author:** Scott Peterson
**URL:** https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Middle-East/2026/0322/hezbollah-existential-war-costs-israel-lebanon-iran
**Date:** March 22, 2026

## Overview

Hezbollah, Iran's most powerful regional ally, has plunged into conflict with Israel following a February 28 U.S. and Israeli attack that assassinated Iran's supreme leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. Despite being weakened by a 2024 Israeli offensive, the organization has rebuilt and rearmed itself over 15 months, surprising observers with its renewed military capabilities.

## Key Developments

A Hezbollah fighter identified as Ahmad describes the organization as "definitely back on its feet" and employing "strategic patience" to restore capabilities. Two days after the U.S.-Israel strike on Iran, Hezbollah joined the war, launching missile and drone attacks into northern Israel.

The escalation has devastated Lebanon: Israeli airstrikes have killed over 1,000 Lebanese and displaced approximately one million people -- roughly one-fifth of the country's population. Israeli ground forces have encountered fierce resistance in southern Lebanon.

## Internal Dynamics

While Hezbollah's rank-and-file view this as an existential "final battle," the conflict remains widely unpopular among Lebanese civilians, including many Hezbollah supporters who resent the scale of destruction. Analysts suggest Iran may have bypassed Hezbollah's more cautious political leadership, including Secretary-General Naim Qassem, to activate military commanders directly.

## Strategic Context

Nicholas Blanford, Atlantic Council expert, notes Hezbollah fighters are "highly motivated" and "delighted" to fight after absorbing sustained Israeli strikes. However, he emphasizes: "They are not going to defeat Israel. They can inflict damage and harm on Israel, but I think this is being led by the Iranians."

## Uncertain Outcomes

Michael Young from the Carnegie Middle East Center warns of a "prolonged situation, a deadlock with no easy solutions," suggesting neither Israeli military action nor Lebanese army efforts will systematically disarm Hezbollah. The conflict risks becoming a protracted insurgency that further devastates Lebanon's already fragile economy and society.
