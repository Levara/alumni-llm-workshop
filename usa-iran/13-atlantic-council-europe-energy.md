# How the Iran War Could Trigger a European Energy Crisis

**Source:** Atlantic Council
**URL:** https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/dispatches/how-the-iran-war-could-trigger-a-european-energy-crisis/
**Date:** March 2026

## The Core Challenge

Europe faces a difficult task refilling gas storage after reducing Russian energy purchases. A closure of the Strait of Hormuz would severely complicate this effort by blocking a major global oil transit route.

## Energy Supply Vulnerability

The Strait of Hormuz represents a critical vulnerability in global energy markets. Disruption to this passage restricts the flow of petroleum supplies that Europe depends on to diversify away from Russian sources. With Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Europe made a strategic pivot away from Russian gas. That pivot relied heavily on LNG imports from Qatar and other Gulf states -- supplies now cut off by the Hormuz closure.

## Storage Concerns

European gas storage facilities are already depleted from previous energy pressures. QatarEnergy's force majeure announcement triggered an abrupt spike in European gas benchmarks, with prices jumping more than 50 percent on March 2 -- the largest single-day increase since the 2022 energy crisis.

Restocking these reserves becomes exponentially harder without reliable supply routes, particularly as geopolitical tensions escalate. Even if a cease-fire were agreed immediately, Europe is likely already heading toward an energy crisis.

## Broader Implications

An Iran conflict creates compounding economic pressures beyond immediate energy costs:

- European industrial capacity threatened by energy shortages
- Economic stability undermined during an already challenging transition period
- The European Central Bank warned that a prolonged conflict will likely trigger stagflation
- Fertilizer supply disruptions threaten agricultural output across the continent

## Strategic Fragility Exposed

The analysis emphasizes that Europe's energy security strategy remains fragile and dependent on maintaining stable Middle Eastern supply chains -- a condition now threatened by escalating regional tensions. The continent's attempt to diversify away from Russian energy has inadvertently created a new vulnerability centered on Gulf state supplies transiting through the Strait of Hormuz.
