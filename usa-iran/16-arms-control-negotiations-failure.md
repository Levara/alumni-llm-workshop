# U.S. Negotiators Were Ill-Prepared for Serious Nuclear Negotiations with Iran

**Source:** Arms Control Association
**URL:** https://www.armscontrol.org/blog/2026-03-11/us-negotiators-were-ill-prepared-serious-nuclear-negotiations-iran
**Date:** March 11, 2026

## Overview

The Arms Control Association published a detailed analysis claiming U.S. Special Envoy Steve Witkoff lacked sufficient technical expertise to effectively negotiate Iran's nuclear program. The piece examines five major areas where Witkoff allegedly misunderstood Iran's positions ahead of the February 2026 strikes.

## Key Findings

The analysis identifies significant technical misstatements by Witkoff regarding Iran's Tehran Research Reactor (TRR). According to the author, Witkoff incorrectly characterized a 45.5 kg stockpile of 20 percent enriched uranium fuel as evidence of weapons development, when in fact this represented roughly 7-8 years of normal operational fuel.

Witkoff apparently dismissed Iran's nuclear proposal based on flawed assumptions about the TRR, viewing enrichment to 20 percent as designed to accelerate weapons development. The author notes this overlooks Iran's stated commitment to avoid accumulating enriched uranium.

## Diplomatic Missteps

The envoy reportedly offered Iran "free fuel" and seemed surprised when rejected, failing to recognize this as fundamentally inconsistent with Iranian sovereignty concerns. The article states Witkoff's background briefings contained additional errors, including:

- Claims about uranium enrichment occurring at the TRR (where it does not occur)
- Mischaracterizations of Iranian centrifuge capabilities
- Failure to understand the distinction between enrichment levels and stockpile quantities

## Three Rounds of Failed Talks

### First Round (February 6, Muscat, Oman)

Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. Iran insisted on addressing solely the nuclear issue, while the U.S. demanded a broader agreement covering missiles and proxies. Iran proposed diluting its 60% enriched uranium stockpile and restoring IAEA access in exchange for sanctions relief.

### Second Round (February 17, Geneva)

Both sides agreed to exchange draft proposals. The U.S. set a month-end deadline. Conflicting accounts emerged about whether the U.S. had demanded "zero enrichment."

### Third Round (February 26, Geneva)

Oman claimed a breakthrough -- Iran allegedly agreed never to stockpile enriched uranium. But interpretations diverged fatally: Iran proposed domestic dilution, while the U.S. insisted on transfer abroad. Two days later, strikes began.

## Conclusion

The Arms Control Association director recommends replacing Witkoff if future diplomatic opportunities arise, citing his insufficient technical preparation as detrimental to U.S. nonproliferation objectives. The failed negotiations are presented as a case study in how diplomatic incompetence can lead to military escalation.
