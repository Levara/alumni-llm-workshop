# After Iran Strikes, Congress Confronts Its Limited Power Over War

**Source:** TIME
**URL:** https://time.com/7381852/iran-war-congress-war-powers-act/
**Date:** March 2026

## Overview

Following President Trump's military operation against Iran that resulted in Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei's death, Congress confronted its limited authority over war decisions made unilaterally by the executive branch.

## The Notification Process

The White House informed the so-called Gang of Eight -- top bipartisan congressional leaders and intelligence committee chairs -- shortly before strikes commenced. Secretary of State Marco Rubio provided advance notice, and administration officials had briefed congressional leadership earlier regarding escalating Iranian tensions. However, these notifications fell short of the constitutional requirement for formal congressional authorization of military action.

## Democratic Concerns

Senator Andy Kim of New Jersey expressed frustration that lawmakers and the public faced military escalation without understanding its endgame. As he stated: "The President has really boxed us in and put us on the hook for things that we haven't discussed."

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer urged Congress to reconvene quickly and reassert constitutional authority. Rank-and-file members from both parties reported receiving minimal information about administration objectives or legal justification before operations commenced.

## The War Powers Response

Congress prepared war powers resolutions to potentially constrain further action:

- A Senate measure, led by Democratic Senator Tim Kaine and backed by Republican Senator Rand Paul, would require explicit authorization before additional Iran hostilities
- A parallel House resolution sought to halt unauthorized military action

However, analysts expect these measures would likely fail to override a presidential veto, functioning primarily as political statements asserting congressional constitutional power rather than immediate operational constraints.

## The Vote

The House narrowly rejected the war powers resolution 212 to 219, largely along party lines. In the Senate, the measure was rejected 53 to 47. Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), one of the sponsors, argued the conflict is not "America First," suggesting fissures within the Republican Party on the war.
