# Threat Brief: March 2026 Escalation of Cyber Risk Related to Iran

**Source:** Unit 42 / Palo Alto Networks
**URL:** https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/iranian-cyberattacks-2026/
**Date:** March 2026

## Executive Summary

Following Operation Epic Fury (U.S.) and Operation Roaring Lion (Israel) launched February 28, 2026, Iran initiated a multi-vector retaliatory cyber campaign. Unit 42 observed escalated cyberattacks from activist groups, though nation-state operations face constraints due to Iran's severely degraded internet connectivity -- dropping to 1-4% availability.

The loss of connectivity and command structure degradation will "likely hinder the ability of state-aligned threat actors to coordinate and execute sophisticated cyberattacks in the near-term." State-aligned units may operate in isolation, while dispersed operators could conduct low-to-medium sophistication disruptions like DDoS attacks and data exfiltration campaigns.

## Current Threat Activity

### Malicious Mobile Campaign

Unit 42 identified an active phishing campaign distributing a malicious Android package impersonating the Israeli Home Front Command RedAlert application, delivering mobile surveillance and data-exfiltrating malware.

### Hacktivist Groups

Approximately 60 individual hacktivist groups became active as of March 2, 2026, including:

**Handala Hack** (MOIS-affiliated): The most prominent Iranian persona, claiming responsibility for compromising Israeli energy companies, Jordan's fuel systems, and Israeli healthcare networks to create domestic pressure.

**APT Iran**: A pro-Iranian collective conducting hack-and-leak operations against Jordan's critical infrastructure.

**Cyber Islamic Resistance**: An umbrella collective coordinating synchronized DDoS attacks, data-wiping operations, and website defacements against Israeli and Western infrastructure.

**Dark Storm Team**: Specializing in large-scale DDoS and ransomware targeting Israeli websites and banking systems.

**FAD Team**: Focused on wiper malware and permanent data destruction, claiming unauthorized access to SCADA/PLC systems across multiple countries.

**Evil Markhors**: Specializing in credential harvesting and targeting Israeli banking systems.

**Sylhet Gang**: Acting as recruitment engine for pro-Iranian hacktivist fronts and participating in DDoS attacks.

**313 Team**: Active pro-Iranian hacktivist cell claiming responsibility for targeting Kuwait Armed Forces, Ministry of Defense, and government websites.

**DieNet**: Conducting DDoS attacks across Middle Eastern targets including airports and financial institutions.

### Escalating Threats

Handala Hack reportedly targeted Iranian-American and Iranian-Canadian influencers with direct death threats via email, "claiming to have leaked their home addresses to physical operatives in their respective home locations." This represents an escalation of threatening cyber activity toward perceived Iran critics.

### Cybercriminal Activity

Threat actors conducted social engineering vishing scams in the United Arab Emirates, impersonating the Ministry of Interior and requesting Emirates Identification Numbers. The ransomware-as-a-service group Tarnished Scorpius listed an Israeli industrial machinery company on its leak site.

### Pro-Russian Hacktivist Activity

Cardinal, a pro-Russian hacktivist group, claimed infiltrating Israeli Defense Forces systems, referencing operational documents. NoName057(16) claimed multiple Israeli targets including municipal and defense-related entities. Russian Legion claimed accessing Israel's Iron Dome missile defense system.

## State-Sponsored Threats

Unit 42 tracks various Iranian state-sponsored actors under constellation name "Serpens." These groups could increase activity in coming weeks, targeting regional entities and supply chains, focusing on destructive and psychological tactics.

## Recommendations

Tactical measures include maintaining offline critical data backups, implementing out-of-band verification for requests, increasing response to threat signals on internet-facing assets, ensuring security patches are current, training employees on phishing tactics, considering geographic IP blocking, and preparing communications plans addressing unauthorized access versus system compromise.

Strategic recommendations involve updating business continuity plans and preparing breach response protocols, noting that threat actors might use claims -- even false ones -- to embarrass victims or disseminate political narratives.
