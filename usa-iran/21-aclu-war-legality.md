# Can Congress Stop President Trump's Illegal War Against Iran?

**Source:** American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU)
**Author:** Christopher Anders, Director of the Democracy and Technology Division
**URL:** https://www.aclu.org/news/civil-liberties/your-questions-answered-can-congress-stop-president-trumps-illegal-war-against-iran
**Date:** March 5, 2026

## Overview

The ACLU examines constitutional war powers following President Trump's announcement on February 28 that the U.S. and Israel began bombing Iran. The article explains that only Congress possesses the constitutional authority to declare war, despite the president's military command responsibilities.

## Key Facts

- At least six U.S. service members and over 1,000 regional civilians have been killed since the bombing began
- Article I of the Constitution grants Congress sole war declaration authority
- The ACLU has maintained this position consistently across administrations for over 50 years

## Constitutional Framework

The Constitution deliberately distributes war powers to prevent concentrated authority. As one expert notes: "The Constitution gives Congress the sole authority to declare war, except in truly exceptional circumstances" like defense against actual attack or imminent danger.

The founders specifically rejected concentrating war-making power in a single individual, having witnessed European monarchs' unchecked military decisions.

## Trump's Justification

Trump has justified the attacks by arguing that despite holding talks with Iran, he believed Tehran was planning to strike first -- thus invoking the "self-defense" justification. The ACLU argues this justification is insufficient for sustained military operations without congressional approval.

## Congressional Remedies

### War Powers Resolution

Congress can invoke this 1973 law requiring the president to terminate unauthorized force if both chambers vote to do so, or after 60 days of undeclared military action. However, the 60-day clock is contested -- administrations have historically argued it doesn't apply to their particular operations.

### Appropriations Authority

Congress controls military funding and can refuse supplemental appropriations bills for unauthorized wars, forcing the administration to seek additional resources through democratic votes. This "power of the purse" represents Congress's most potent constitutional tool.

## Historical Context

The article documents how war declarations historically enabled domestic civil liberties violations, including:

- Espionage Act enforcement during WWI (jailing critics of the war)
- Japanese American incarceration during WWII
- Post-9/11 surveillance and detention programs (Guantanamo, warrantless wiretapping)

The ACLU argues that the lack of formal congressional authorization makes the current military action constitutionally suspect and sets dangerous precedents for executive overreach.
