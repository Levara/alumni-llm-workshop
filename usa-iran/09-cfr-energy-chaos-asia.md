# The Iran War is Causing Energy Chaos in Asia

**Source:** Council on Foreign Relations (CFR)
**Authors:** Joshua Kurlantzick and Annabel Richter
**URL:** https://www.cfr.org/articles/the-iran-war-is-causing-energy-chaos-in-asia
**Date:** March 18, 2026

## Overview

Following U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran, the country's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz has created severe energy supply disruptions across Asia, pushing the region toward potential economic collapse and social unrest.

## Current Crisis Dimensions

Oil futures closed at $95 per barrel as major Gulf oil and gas facilities suffered damage. Strikes targeted:

- Ras Tanura refinery (Saudi Arabia)
- Ras Laffan gas processing base (Qatar)
- Ruwais refinery complex (UAE)

Combined with Iran's Strait of Hormuz blockade, Gulf oil production has dropped by 10 million barrels daily compared to March 2025. Oil flow through the Strait has plummeted to "less than 10 percent of pre-war levels."

## Asia's Vulnerability

In 2024, "84% of the oil and 83% of the liquified natural gas (LNG)" transiting the Strait was destined for Asia. Major economies -- China, Japan, Singapore, Taiwan, South Korea, India, and Thailand -- depend almost entirely on foreign oil supplies.

## Government Responses

Asian nations have implemented aggressive conservation measures:

- **Philippines:** Four-day workweeks; computers switched off during lunch; air conditioning set to minimum 24C (75F)
- **Vietnam:** Work-from-home encouragement
- **Thailand:** Officials urged to use stairs instead of elevators
- **Multiple nations:** Fuel rationing; strategic reserve releases

## Economic Damage

Factories operate part-time or have closed entirely. Tourism -- critical for Thailand, Vietnam, and Singapore -- has collapsed. Thailand tourist arrivals fell approximately 9 percent year-on-year in early March, with hotels reporting occupancy rates as low as 10 percent.

## Subsidy Crises

Governments are absorbing fuel price increases, but unsustainably:

- **Indonesia:** Subsidies threaten budget deficits exceeding the legal 3 percent GDP cap
- **India:** "The scale of demand in India, the world's most populous nation, limits how long it can cap prices"

## Political Risk

Historical precedent warns of danger. Indonesia's 1998 uprising -- partly triggered by fuel price spikes during financial crisis -- ultimately toppled the Suharto regime. Current protests have already turned violent in some nations. Bangladesh has closed universities and deployed military personnel to guard oil depots.

## Outlook

The authors warn: "If it lasts through the summer, it could have calamitous effects on Asian growth and political stability." The war, merely twenty days old at publication, threatens both economic devastation and unprecedented regional instability.
